Calculator skills the exam expects

On your graphing calculator, practice:

  • Graphing a function in a window you choose
  • Finding zeros (calc → zero)
  • Finding intersections of two graphs (calc → intersect)
  • Finding relative max and min
  • Regressions: linear, quadratic, cubic, quartic, exponential, logarithmic
  • Evaluating a function at a value and using a table

Calculator habits that save points

  • Radian mode for the AP exam
  • Store values (STO→) instead of retyping rounded numbers
  • Round to 3 decimal places only in the final answer
  • Write the setup on paper before pressing buttons: the setup earns points

Study methods that actually work

  • Active recall: say the answer before flipping a flashcard
  • Spacing: short sessions over several days beat one long cram
  • Mixing: shuffle topics in practice (the "All 90" set does this)

Glossary

Read this the night before

One-page cheat sheet

Rates of change

  • Average rate of change on [a, b]: (f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a)
  • Linear ⇔ constant 1st differences
  • Quadratic ⇔ constant 2nd differences
  • Degree n ⇔ constant nth differences
  • Concave up ⇔ rate of change increasing
  • Concave down ⇔ rate of change decreasing

Polynomials

  • Degree n ⇒ exactly n complex zeros (with multiplicity)
  • Complex zeros come in pairs: a + bi and a − bi
  • Odd multiplicity: graph crosses. Even: graph touches and bounces
  • Even degree: both ends the same way. Odd degree: ends opposite
  • Even function: f(−x) = f(x), symmetric about the y-axis
  • Odd function: f(−x) = −f(x), symmetric about the origin

Rational functions

  • Factor first. Cancelled factor ⇒ hole. Leftover zero of denominator ⇒ vertical asymptote
  • Zeros: zeros of the numerator that are in the domain
  • deg top < deg bottom ⇒ HA y = 0
  • deg top = deg bottom ⇒ HA y = ratio of leading coefficients
  • deg top = deg bottom + 1 ⇒ slant asymptote (long division)
  • deg top > deg bottom ⇒ no HA, behaves like the leading-term ratio

Transformations

  • a·f(x): vertical stretch by |a|, a < 0 reflects over the x-axis
  • f(bx): horizontal stretch by 1/|b|, b < 0 reflects over the y-axis
  • f(x − h): shift right h
  • f(x) + k: shift up k
  • Inside the parentheses does the opposite of what you'd guess

Sequences & exponentials

  • Arithmetic: aₙ = a₀ + dn (add d)
  • Geometric: gₙ = g₀·rⁿ (multiply by r)
  • f(x) = a·bˣ: b > 1 growth, 0 < b < 1 decay
  • Growth by r% ⇒ b = 1 + r. Decay by r% ⇒ b = 1 − r
  • b^(x+k) = bᵏ·bˣ (a horizontal shift is a vertical stretch)
  • Change units: a·bᵗ = a·(b^(1/12))^(12t)

Logarithms

  • log_b(c) = a ⇔ bᵃ = c
  • log(xy) = log x + log y
  • log(x/y) = log x − log y
  • log(xⁿ) = n·log x
  • log_b x = ln x / ln b
  • Domain of log_b(x): x > 0. VA at x = 0
  • Always check your solutions in the original log equation

Composition & inverses

  • f(g(x)): do g first, then f
  • Inverse: swap x and y, solve for y
  • (a, b) on f ⇔ (b, a) on f⁻¹; reflection over y = x
  • Only one-to-one functions have inverses; you may need to restrict the domain
  • Domain of f⁻¹ = range of f

Semi-log plots

  • y-axis on a log scale makes exponential data look linear
  • If log y = mx + c, then y = 10ᶜ·(10ᵐ)ˣ
  • If ln y = mx + c, then y = eᶜ·(eᵐ)ˣ

Exam day

Seven moves that save points

Never leave an MCQ blank. There is no penalty for guessing. Cross out two wrong answers and guess between the last two.

Plug in the answer choices. On "solve" questions, testing A–D in the original equation is often faster than solving.

Factor before you do anything else with a rational function. Holes and asymptotes jump out once it's factored.

On FRQs, write the equation before the number. "R(0) = 75 and R(3) = 70.84" earns a point by itself. A bare decimal often earns nothing.

Use units in every interpretation. "On average, subscribers increased by 0.906 million per year from t = 1 to t = 3." Name the quantity, the units and the interval.

Give 3 decimal places on calculator answers, and keep full precision in your calculator until the end.
